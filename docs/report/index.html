Screwworm zone freedom model (draft)

What the tool estimates: the model estimate of the chance (draft) that a control zone is free of New World screwworm over time after its last detection, given that surveillance finds nothing more. It is a proof-of-concept model.

Do not cite these numbers as evidence of absence or freedom, for release, trade or reporting. They are outputs of a draft model under assumed rates. APHIS declares freedom by time since the last detection, with fly trapping and animal inspection; this model has no fly trapping (what the model leaves out).

What "free" means: absolute absence, that is, no infected unit in the population. This is distinct from effective absence, where prevalence is below a chosen design prevalence. The model follows the zero-inflated freedom model (Preventive Veterinary Medicine, 2026, 106892). For screwworm the unit is a farm: the zone is free when none of its farms is infested. Where a card says "freedom", it means this model estimate, not freedom as APHIS declares it (the APHIS criterion).

The model in brief

The zone is a set of farms. New infested farms arrive from outside at an assumed rate. Inside an infested farm the infestation passes from animal to animal until it runs its course. Two surveillance streams look for it: owners who notice a wound and report it, and inspection rounds that examine animals on a sample of farms. Each day with no confirmed case lowers the chance that an infestation is present; new arrivals keep adding to it.

Surveillance streams: which the model uses

In the model:

  • Owner reports: run all the time: an owner notices a visible wound and the case becomes a confirmed detection after the reporting delay, which also covers the veterinarian's visit and the laboratory.
  • Inspection rounds: every 30 days, 20 of the 100 farms, 30 animals examined on each; an animal carrying larvae is found with the inspection sensitivity.

Not in the model:

  • Fly trapping: not modelled, so trap results add no evidence; it is a named later addition to the model, not built.
  • Wildlife surveillance (deer, feral swine, hunter reports): wildlife enters only as part of the introduction rate; the model has no wildlife surveillance stream, so wildlife reports add no evidence in the model, although they happen in practice.
  • Movement checks (pre-movement inspection and treatment at checkpoints and markets): not modelled; moved animals enter only as part of the introduction rate.
  • Slaughter inspection: not in the model's design.
  • Pets, holdings under 10 animals and human cases: not surveyed; outside the model's farms.

Not in the sensitivity ranking: the starting state, the farm design and the surveillance design above are fixed in every draw, so which parameters matter says nothing about them. Their effect can be large: in a separate model run with only herd size changed (six-month scenario, other values at their centrals), herds of 30 animals instead of 100 lowered the six-month estimate from about 0.93 to about 0.88.

Fly trapping and season are not in the model: see what the model leaves out.

What the model leaves out

  • Fly trapping: not in the model; trap results add no evidence here.
  • Season: the rates come from summer 2026 and are held constant; a six-month horizon runs into winter, when fly activity falls.
  • Wildlife and pets: the model has no surveillance stream for them; they enter only through the introduction rate, so reports about them add no evidence in the model, although such reports do happen in practice. The model says nothing about freedom in wildlife.
  • Herds under 10 animals: outside the model's farms.
  • Pupae and the next fly generation: a farm stops seeding others when its animals stop carrying larvae; in the field, flies emerge about a week after the larvae drop and lay eggs about a week after that.
  • Controls after the last detection: quarantine, movement controls and sterile-fly releases are not states of the model.
  • Spread between farms inside a free zone: spread between farms acts only once a farm in the zone is infested. Spread from nearby infested areas enters through the introduction interval.
  • Introduction pathways: the introduction interval is one zone-level rate covering flies, moved animals and wildlife. Weather (temperature and rain), the frequency and amount of sterile-fly release, and preventive treatment change arrivals in practice but do not change the rate in the model. Sterile-fly release enters only as part of the cut in between-farm spread after a detection.
  • Confirmation: a detection means a laboratory-confirmed case; false alarms are not modelled.

Freedom in the model and in an APHIS declaration

In the model, the zone is free when none of its 100 farms is infested. This is not the APHIS release or freedom criterion. The APHIS response strategy (the Green Book, A-1.1, page 25 of the PDF) says:

A declaration of NWS freedom after an infestation in the United States would be based on surveillance for NWS that includes trapping of flies and visual inspection of animals for myiasis. Depending on specific weather and seasonal conditions in the area(s) of infestation, surveillance should be carried out for 3 months past the last NWS detection

and gives that as generally 3 to 4 fly life cycles during an outbreak. The model's probability is at most a supporting quantity beside that, not a substitute for it.

The model's equations

The core of the model in plain notation, written out from its design document. Rates are per day; a time on the page (days) becomes a rate as 1 / time, or ln 2 / time for a doubling time.

The zone: farms becoming infested and clearing

new infestations per day: b(K, t) = ((M − K) / M) × (λ + βeff(t) × K)
leaving the count: each farm at age τend, the first age at which N (πL(τ) + πI(τ)) < 1/2
after the first confirmed detection, also: d(K) = γrel × K
spread between farms: βeff(t) = l(t) × βmax × p̄shed(t)

Before any detection a farm leaves K only when its own infestation has run its course: 166 days after infestation at the central values. Each farm is fully counted until then, then gone; there is no variation between farms in when an infestation ends. Zone clearance, a steady exit whether or not the farm was found, is off in this scenario.

Mnumber of farms in the zone
K(t)number of infested farms at day t
λ = 1 / introduction intervalrate of new arrivals from outside, for the zone as a whole (it does not grow with M)
τendage at which a farm's own infestation has run its course (fewer than half an animal expected still infested); the farm leaves the count then, found or not (166 days at the central values)
γrel = 1 / release timeafter the first confirmed detection, rate at which each counted farm leaves (21 days on average, the zone clearance card's value, from the release rule for found and treated farms)
βmax = ln 2 / zone doubling timespread between farms with no control
l(t)the lockdown multiplier once a detection has been confirmed, otherwise 1
p̄shed(t)average share of animals carrying larvae, over the ages of the farms infested at day t

Inside one infested farm

dπL/dτ = βw πI πS − γL πL
dπI/dτ = γL πL − γR πI
dπR/dτ = γR πI, with πS = 1 − πL − πI − πR
share carrying larvae: pshed(τ) = πI(τ)
share with a visible wound: pclin(τ) = fmax (1 − e−τ/Tc) πI(τ)

Every infested farm runs this same course; there is no fly population and no pupal stage.

τdays since the farm was infested
πS, πL, πI, πRshares of the farm's animals: not infested, eggs laid but no larvae visible, carrying larvae, recovered (treated, healed or dead)
βw = ln 2 / within-premises doubling timespread between animals on the farm
γL = 1 / latent timeeggs laid to larvae visible
γR = 1 / within-premises recovery timean animal stops carrying larvae
fmax, Tcshare of wounds visible, and the clinical onset time

Detection

a visited farm passes inspection clean with chance E[(1 − Se)J], where X ~ Binomial(N, pshed(τ)) and J ~ Hypergeometric(N, X, n)
round r is all clean with chance Lr(K): the product of those chances over the infested farms among the visited ones
owner reports from a farm of age τ, per day: h(τ) = ρr × [1 − (1 − ρa pclin(τ))N]

Detection means a confirmed case; false alarms are not modelled. The first detection sets the lockdown multiplier and the release clock on for the rest of the horizon.

N, nanimals on a farm, and animals examined per visited farm
Seinspection sensitivity: chance an inspector finds an examined animal that carries larvae
X, Janimals carrying larvae on the farm, and how many of them are among the n examined (drawn without replacement)
ρachance the owner notices a visible wound (per animal)
ρrowner reporting rate per day, about 1 / mean reporting delay
Lr(K)chance inspection round r finds nothing when K farms are infested

The freedom calculation

Pfree(t) = P(K(t) = 0 | no confirmed detection through day t)

The chance of each value of K is carried forward in time. After each clean inspection round it is multiplied by Lr(K) and rescaled to sum to 1 (Bayes' rule); between rounds the same update runs continuously for "no owner report". At the start, K(0) is drawn from a Poisson distribution and each starting farm's age from an exponential distribution (see the six-month scenario).

Two implementations compute these same equations: an analytic calculation and a simulator of random histories (the agreement check).

Do two independent builds agree?

The interactive app runs the same analytic calculation as the numbers on this page.

Two implementations written independently from the same written design, an analytic (closed-form) calculation and a simulator (10,000 random histories per setting), were run on 471 comparisons: 94 parameter settings, nearly all read at five times from 30 to 180 days. The test grid is centred on a fixed set of test values, not on this page's six-month scenario. 53 could not be compared: 5 comparisons were lost to a simulator time-out, and 48 had nothing to compare against because no simulated history came through with clean surveillance data or the simulator's spread was zero.

Of the 418 that were compared, 1 gap was larger than three simulator standard deviations, against about 1.1 expected by chance (2 allowed). Reruns show this one is not chance: a small bias in the analytic calculation of about 0.004 at the extreme within-farm doubling time of 0.1 days, far below the parameter's range. The analytic calculation sat above the simulator in 196 comparisons and below it in 222 (sign test p = 0.22: no clear lean either way).

A separate check of 38 settings around the page's own six-month scenario found one gap: with no surveillance at all, an old leftover farm and spread between farms on, the analytic calculation reads about 0.008 low. No setting in that check with surveillance shows that gap; the checks bound the bias at the settings tested, not everywhere.

Agreement says the arithmetic matches the design. It does not say the design or the numbers are right.

How to read the ranking

  • Range: everywhere on this page a parameter's range is its 90 percent interval: values were drawn so that 5 percent fall below the low end and 5 percent above the high end (a lognormal, Gamma or Beta curve fitted to the two ends). Most range ends are judgement; each card says which.
  • Variance share: the share of the spread in the six-month estimate across the draws that the parameter explains on its own, estimated from averages in 10 equal bins.
  • A wide range ranks high by construction: the ranking measures each range as set, and most ranges are judgement.
  • Conditional on the scenario: the starting state, the farm design and the surveillance design are fixed (see the six-month scenario), so the ranking says nothing about them; herds of 30 animals instead of 100 lower the six-month estimate from about 0.93 to about 0.88.

Introduction interval

What this is: The average number of days between new infestations arriving in the zone from outside it (flies flying in, moved animals or wildlife), one rate for the whole zone. In the zone picture it is the "New arrivals from outside" input. It counts arrivals from outside the zone. As fitted, it also carries spread from infested areas next to the zone (see how the value was reached); the zone's own spread term is separate and has almost no effect on freedom.

Value: 130 d, range 50 to 400 d (90 percent interval); average effect on six-month freedom +0.117.

What the value rests on: counted from reports First cases counted from news reports and a public case compilation, then divided by a window and a judged number of at-risk counties; fails the page's rule for a high-impact parameter.

How the value was reached

The chosen 130 days (range 50 to 400) is for a free county next to an active zone. The AI literature review dated every Texas county's first case in summer 2026 [C-2.1, C-2.2, C-2.7a, C-2.7b]. Owner reporting delay

What this is: How fast a wound the owner has noticed becomes a confirmed detection, set as a daily rate; one over the rate is the mean delay in days. In the model pictures it is part of "Owner reports a wound" on the farm picture and the "Owner reports" input on the zone picture. What the delay covers

The domain experts break the time from a fly laying eggs to a confirmed case into four parts. The model lumps the last three into this one delay, so it covers more than the owner's wait:

  1. Fly to noticing: from egg laying until the owner sees a wound. Not in this delay: the latent time and the chance a sick animal is noticed cover it.
  2. Owner waits: the owner has seen something but looks closely days later. In this delay.
  3. Veterinary sampling: a veterinarian or official comes out to sample and ships it. In this delay.
  4. Laboratory: from receipt to confirmation, "several days" (domain experts; no written source). One 2026 preprint reports 3 to 7 day testing bottlenecks (Garcia, abstract only, a background claim, not a measurement). In this delay.

The parts are not built up into the 3 days.

Abstract

Eradicating screwworm, Cochliomyia hominivorax (Coquerel), from continental North American via the sterile insect technique has provided huge economic benefit to livestock producers by eliminating screwworm myiasis. After confirmatory identification of fly samples from infested deer by the USDA National Veterinary Services Laboratory on September 30, 2016, an alert was issued that screwworm myiasis was discovered in the Florida Keys. Personnel from USDA Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service, Agricultural Research Service, the State of Florida, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and local officials responded to the outbreak focus on Big Pine Key. After witnessing infested Key deer (Odocoileus virginianus clavium Barboyr & Allen), screwworm adult sampling was initiated at 0930 h on October 5, 2016 using nets to collect flies arriving at putrid liver, with the first female collected within 1 h. Larval samples were collected from infested animals for DNA analyses and to develop a "Florida outbreak" colony to test mating compatibility with the mass-produced strain used for sterile fly releases. Ground release chambers for sterile screwworm releases were placed in favorable habitats based on satellite image analyses. Sterile pupae were first placed in the chambers on October 11, 2016. Further liver trapping showed that 13 Keys were infested. One case, presumably through animal movement, occurred near Homestead on the Florida mainland. Ultimately there were 35 sterile fly release stations, including 4 located around Homestead, but no further cases were identified. About 188 million sterile flies were released until successful eradication was declared on March 23, 2017. Containing the outbreak prevented economic losses to livestock producers and other wildlife on the mainland and kept eradication costs to a minimum.
